Privacy Policy

Futari Productions · Last updated: November 25, 2026

This policy explains what information our games use, why, and the choices you have. It covers every game Futari Productions publishes. Each game has its own section below, because each game is different.

Who we are

Futari Productions (formerly Puzzle Me Studio) is an independent game developer. If you have any question about this policy or your data, write to us at hi@futariproductions.com.

What we never do

We do not sell your personal information. We do not ask you to create an account, and we do not collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts through our games. Your game progress is saved on your own device.

Your choices and rights

  • Withdraw or change consent: where a game asks for your consent (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), you can change your choice at any time from the game's Settings screen. The section for each game explains how.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete your device's advertising ID in your Android settings.
  • Access and deletion: you can ask us what data we hold about you, or ask us to delete it, by writing to hi@futariproductions.com. Our games have no accounts, so tell us which game and roughly when you played. We will delete the data we control and explain what we cannot reach. Uninstalling a game deletes everything it saved on your device.
  • If you write to us by email, we use your email address and message only to reply to you.

Children

Our games are not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when our games change. When we do, we will change the "Last updated" date above.

Meowtide

Meowtide is a cozy game about cats on a small island. There is no account and no login. Your island, your cats and the photos of story moments you take in the game are stored only on your device. Garden reminders are local notifications created on your device; we run no notification server.

Meowtide uses three services from Google:

1. Google AdMob (rewarded ads)

Meowtide only shows rewarded ads, and only when you choose to watch one by tapping a button. There are no banner ads and no ads that interrupt play. To load and show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares with Google:

  • your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location (approximate location);
  • your interactions with the app and the ads, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage;
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent form before any ad is loaded, and Settings has a "Privacy choices" button that opens the form again so you can change your choice at any time.

2. Google Play Billing (in-app purchase)

Meowtide sells one optional in-app item, which lets the helper kitten stay forever. Payments are handled entirely by Google Play. We never see your card number or other payment details. The game only learns which item your Google Play account owns, so it can unlock it.

3. Google Analytics for Firebase (analytics)

To understand how the game is played and improve it, the game sends a small set of events to Google Analytics for Firebase: when a play session starts (with the number of days played), which screens and main pop-ups are opened, which tutorial steps are completed or skipped, when a battle starts and ends (stage, mode, result, wave reached, monsters defeated, time and the reward chosen), when a stage is cleared, which skills and evolutions were picked, upgrades bought, features unlocked, the in-game currencies earned and spent (which currency, how much, and from or on what part of the game), chests opened, Star Wheel spins, Festival, pass and veteran-pack rewards claimed, which Settings switches you change, when a rewarded ad is requested, shown, watched to its reward, closed early or fails to show, when a full-screen ad is shown or skipped, the estimated revenue each ad earned (an amount in currency, with the ad format and ad unit, never tied to your name), and which in-app offers were shown, tapped or closed and how a purchase ended (completed, pending, cancelled or failed; no prices or payment details). It also stores five settings about your game: the highest stage reached, whether the game lowered its frame rate for your device, whether you have ever made a purchase, whether you own Remove Ads and how many rewarded ads you have watched. Google Analytics records sessions automatically, together with an app-instance identifier, the advertising ID and an approximate location derived from a masked IP address. We never send your save file or anything you type. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, analytics only runs if you agree to it in the consent form.

All of this data is encrypted in transit. Google processes it under its own policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Memory Card Puzzle (The Chosen One)

From version 3.0.0, the Google Play app Memory Card Puzzle becomes a new game, The Chosen One: a hero defends a small arena against waves of monsters. Versions before 3.0.0 show no ads, use no analytics and collect no personal information. The rest of this section describes version 3.0.0 and later.

There is no account and no login. Your progress (stages, upgrades, equipment, pets, purchases and settings) is stored only on your device. The game uses these services from Google and Unity:

1. Google AdMob (ads)

The game shows two kinds of ads. Rewarded ads play only when you choose to watch one by tapping a button, for example to double a reward. Full-screen ads may appear between battles, after some finished battles, never during a battle. There are no banner ads. To load and show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares with Google:

  • your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location (approximate location);
  • your interactions with the app and the ads, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage;
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and the app set ID.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent form before any ad is loaded, and a "Privacy" button in the game opens the form again so you can change your choice at any time.

2. Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Remote Config

To understand how the game is played and improve it, the game sends a small set of events to Google Analytics for Firebase: when a play session starts (with the number of days played), when a battle starts and ends (stage, mode, result, wave reached, monsters defeated, time and the reward chosen), when a stage is cleared, which skills and evolutions were picked, upgrades bought, features unlocked, when a rewarded ad is requested or earned and a full-screen ad is shown, and which in-app offers were shown or tapped and how a purchase ended (completed, pending, cancelled or failed; no prices or payment details). It also stores four settings about your game: the highest stage reached, whether the game lowered its frame rate for your device, whether you have ever made a purchase and whether you own Remove Ads. Google Analytics records sessions automatically, together with an app-instance identifier, the advertising ID and an approximate location derived from a masked IP address. We never send your save file or anything you type. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, analytics only runs if you agree to it in the consent form.

Firebase Remote Config lets us adjust game settings, such as how often full-screen ads may appear, without an app update. To fetch them, the game sends Google an app-instance identifier and basic device information (app version, language, country).

3. Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports)

When the game crashes or hits an error, Firebase Crashlytics (from Google) sends a report so we can find and fix the bug. A report contains the error and a stack trace (where in the game's code it happened), the device model, the Android version, the app version, a Crashlytics installation identifier, and the game screen and stage you were on at the time. Crash reporting is part of keeping the game working, so it runs for everyone and is not tied to your choice in the consent form. It is not used for advertising and is not sold. The reports are processed by Google on our behalf and are not shared with anyone else.

4. In-app purchases (Google Play Billing and Unity In-App Purchasing)

The game sells optional items for real money: Remove Ads (removes full-screen ads; rewarded ads stay optional), starter packs, gem packs, stage supply packs and Festival packs. Payments are handled entirely by Google Play: we never see your card or payment details. When you buy something, the game receives the order from Google Play (product, order and purchase token, and a receipt signed by Google), checks the signature on your device, gives you the item and keeps the order number on your device so it is never given twice. If you reinstall the game, Remove Ads comes back automatically from your Google Play account; other items are not restored.

The game uses Unity In-App Purchasing, a library made by Unity Technologies, to talk to Google Play. This library sends Unity a record of each purchase step (started, paid, failed or completed) with the product, the order number, price, currency and country, the Google receipt, and basic device information (device model, operating system, language, time of install, app version). It does not send your advertising ID. Unity processes this data under its own privacy policy.

5. Time check

To stop daily rewards being claimed early by changing the device clock, the game asks www.google.com (or, if that fails, www.cloudflare.com) for the current time when it starts and when you return to it. The request sends no data from the game; like any web request, it reveals your IP address to that service.

All of this data is encrypted in transit. Google and Unity process it under their own policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • Firebase privacy and data disclosure (including Crashlytics): https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy
  • Unity Privacy Policy: https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy
